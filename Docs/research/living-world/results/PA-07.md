## 0. Question, authority and recommendation

PA-07 asks:

> What is the smallest work/service/material model that makes absence, closure, capacity or a relevant resource shortage alter human opportunities and later choices, without building an invisible economy game?

**Recommendation:** model **human-facing service opportunity and a deliberately tiny set of decision-relevant material dependencies**, not a miniature macroeconomy.

The minimum useful causal spine is:

```text
role/service requirement + opening/operating condition
                    ↓
current staffing / capacity / named dependency state
                    ↓
service or work opportunity becomes available, degraded or unavailable
                    ↓
PA-01 expected routine meets the actual opportunity
                    ↓
PA-02 actor chooses / fails / waits / substitutes / replans
                    ↓
material/service state and other owned consequences may change
                    ↓
other actors later face a changed opportunity
```

Work does **not** own an NPC's timetable or decision. Employment/structural social ties remain PA-03-owned; PA-01 owns expected routine semantics; PA-02 owns actor choice. PA-07 owns the material/service truth that those systems are allowed to encounter.

This is a research finding, not a runtime schema, economic balance, Unity proof or final content catalogue.

## 2. Minimum semantic vocabulary

These are semantic responsibilities, **not API/type names**.

### 2.1 Work/service requirement

A place/service may declare a bounded operating requirement such as:

- an eligible role/person is present or available;
- an opening/service window is active;
- one or more service-capacity slots are usable;
- a named material dependency is satisfied;
- required access/place/equipment is available.

This describes what the service needs, not which actor must choose to satisfy it.

### 2.2 Service state

A service/opportunity must be able to be meaningfully:

- `AVAILABLE`;
- `DEGRADED` when reduced capacity/offer still has gameplay value;
- `UNAVAILABLE`.

The reason is structured and actor/tooling-visible only through appropriate access:

```text
closed window
missing staffing
capacity occupied
named resource unavailable
required place/equipment unavailable
policy/access condition
```

The exact enum/storage is deferred. The product requirement is that “closed/no capacity” cannot remain secretly selectable as normal service.

### 2.3 Capacity / claimable opportunity

Capacity exists only where contention can change behaviour: a staffed counter, limited table/equipment, one delivery opportunity, finite appointment/service slots, etc.

PA-07 does **not** require every chair, glass or shelf item to become an economy entity. Capacity should be represented at the coarsest level that preserves the relevant conflict and trace.

### 2.4 Named material dependency

Persist a material/resource concept only when its presence/absence/quantity band can alter:

- eligibility;
- service availability/capacity;
- a later actor choice;
- a recoverable consequence;
- a player-meaningful intervention.

A dependency should normally be coarse: for example `bar-service-stock available/depleted`, not every bottle as a simulated commodity, unless a future gameplay feature requires individual objects.

### 2.5 Demand and supply at town scale

Juego2 does not need a global market-clearing model for PA-07.

Use **local opportunity pressure** instead:

- demand = actual bounded actors/requests/commitments seeking a service/resource/opportunity;
- supply = current local service/resource/capacity that can satisfy them;
- contention = more eligible claims than available capacity;
- unsatisfied demand = an explicit failure/wait/substitution input to actor agency.

No global population/economy scan is required to know that two people want one available slot.

### 2.6 Substitution and recovery

When a worker/resource is unavailable, valid outcomes include:

- another **eligible actor chooses** to cover;
- reduced service/capacity;
- wait/retry later;
- an explicitly authored compatible substitute resource/service;
- actor chooses another place/action;
- closure until the dependency is restored.

Forbidden baseline: instant invisible replacement that restores full service without a choice/cost/consequence whenever a person/resource is missing.

## 4. Required causal scenarios

Detailed fixtures live in `Docs/evidence/WP-PA-B1/BATCH_CAUSAL_FIXTURES.md`. The decisive PA-07 scenario uses a compact bar/service fixture because it composes directly with PA-08/09 without making those units prerequisites for PA-07's core result.

### P07-1 — Worker absence changes multiple choices

Baseline:

- Bar F01 has an active service window.
- Carmen is the expected service worker.
- Antonio intends to use the service before a later commitment.
- Manolo has a compatible fallback elsewhere but with greater travel/time cost.

Perturbation:

- Carmen becomes legitimately unavailable for the service window.

Required causal outcome:

1. PA-07 service capacity changes; no invisible substitute restores it.
2. Antonio encounters degraded/unavailable service and PA-02 chooses wait/fallback/change activity according to his own inputs.
3. Manolo's later option/claim changes because Antonio's fallback competes for or changes another bounded opportunity, **or** a second worker independently chooses whether to cover the missing capacity.
4. At least two actor decisions differ from the baseline because of shared service/material state, not authored quest branches.

The exact actors/content are fixtures; the semantic requirement is multi-actor consequence through shared state.

### P07-2 — Material shortage with recovery

A named `bar-service-stock` dependency becomes unavailable. The bar can degrade or close an affected service. An eligible delivery/helper can later restore that dependency through an ordinary world action. Recovery changes opportunity; it does not rewind actors or erase consequences that already happened.

### P07-3 — Household/work conflict

An actor has a work obligation and an incompatible social/material commitment in the same window. PA-07 exposes both work requirement and current material consequence; PA-01/02/03 supply schedule, choice and obligation inputs. No unique NPC script resolves the conflict.

## 5. Player and governance seams

### Player

PA-07 intentionally exposes a material seam that PA-09 can use later. Valid player interventions may include, when authorized by content:

- deliver/return a relevant resource;
- help unload/setup/repair a bounded service dependency;
- occupy/release a scarce opportunity;
- take/steal/block a relevant resource with normal ownership/consequence semantics;
- reroute a dependency by enabling an accepted substitute.

The player does not receive privileged mutation rights. A semantically equivalent NPC action must be capable of entering the same material owner.

### Governance

A future governance owner may alter:

- service/opening rules;
- access/permission;
- public resource allocation;
- staffing/service constraints where product-appropriate.

PA-07 receives those as changed conditions. Governance must not directly choose the affected actor's reaction.

## 6. Mechanism decisions

| Mechanism | Disposition | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Explicit service availability/degradation | **ADOPT** | Makes business state causally meaningful and inspectable. |
| Staffing as one possible service requirement | **ADOPT** | Absence must be able to matter. |
| Local finite capacity where contention is visible | **ADOPT** | Produces decisions without global economy simulation. |
| Coarse named material dependencies tied to behaviour | **ADOPT** | Enables player/world intervention while controlling state size. |
| Actor-owned cover/substitution decision | **ADOPT** | Preserves PA-02 and makes people matter. |
| Explicit failure/wait/fallback | **ADOPT** | Prevents magic service and hidden repair. |
| Local request/claim pressure instead of global demand curve | **ADOPT** | Appropriate to compact-town gameplay and bounded discovery. |
| Full price formation / inflation / macro supply-demand model | **REJECT by default** | No B1 acceptance scenario needs it. Add only if a future concrete gameplay claim does. |
| Per-object commodity bookkeeping | **REJECT by default** | State/authoring cost without demonstrated behaviour value. |
| Instant invisible worker replacement | **REJECT** | Makes individual absence causally meaningless. |
| Economy-owned schedule/actor control | **REJECT** | Duplicates PA-01/02 authority. |
| Universal money score for social choices | **REJECT** | Flattens typed social/material reasons. |
| Exact counts, prices, throughput and inventory tuning | **LATER / empirical** | Requires H3/H4+ content/runtime observation. |

## 7. Acceptance and hard negative

PA-07's acceptance claim is satisfied at research level because the proposed model can represent a bounded staffing/resource change whose shared service state alters at least two actors' later choices, with the choices still owned by PA-02.

**Hard negative — hidden macroeconomy:** propose a town GDP, commodity price curve, global demand index or per-item inventory ledger. Remove it from all PA-07 causal fixtures. If no actor opportunity, decision, consequence or player-visible trace changes, that state is not part of the minimum PA-07 model.

This is stronger than “macro simulation is bad”: it is a deletion test for necessity.

## 9. Residuals allowed by this research result

- Some businesses may require no simulated stock at all if opening/staff/capacity already creates the useful consequence.
- Some jobs may remain visually abstract when their internal work has no player-facing causal value.
- A future concrete feature may justify a richer resource or money mechanic, but it must pass the same behaviour-value test rather than inheriting complexity automatically.
- Cultural/business content is not selected by PA-07.

## 10. Unit conclusion

PA-07 recommends **services and opportunities first, dependencies only when behaviour-visible, economy last**.

The work/material layer earns state only when it can answer a gameplay question such as:

> Who can do what now, what became unavailable, who notices/depends on it, what can restore or worsen it, and which later actor choice changed because of that fact?

If a proposed economic variable cannot answer one of those questions in the intended town scale, it stays out of the minimum model.
