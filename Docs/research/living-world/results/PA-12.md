## 0. Question and recommendation

PA-12 asks:

> What minimal governance model lets the player reshape the town at high leverage while consequences still belong to the same material, social, causal and actor-owned Living World?

**Recommendation:** governance should publish **explicit institutional condition changes** into existing opportunity/state owners, then get out of the way.

```text
legitimate municipal decision
        ↓
explicit rule / access / schedule / capacity / allocation / service condition
        ↓
affected actors encounter the changed condition
        ↓
PA-02 actors independently choose / refuse / adapt / exploit / resist
        ↓
normal material, activity, social, information and event owners carry consequences
        ↓
player encounters aftermath in third-person play
        ↓
player may help / obstruct / mediate / amend / repeal through accepted actions
        ↓
new equilibrium, recovery, backlash or deliberate transformation
```

The mayor is allowed to have **large scope**. Large scope is not mind control. The policy changes what is possible/permitted/available; private belief, relationship and action remain owned elsewhere.

## 2. Minimal municipal lever vocabulary

These are **product-semantic families**, not promised UI buttons or API classes.

### 2.1 Access and permission

Examples: use of a public place, permit/closure condition, temporary restriction, exception or priority access.

Authority changes eligibility/access. It does not choose whether a citizen then attends, protests, complies or ignores the opportunity.

### 2.2 Time and schedule conditions

Examples: service hours, public-event windows, municipal staffing availability, market/festival timing.

A time rule changes the opportunity environment. PA-01/02 still own expected routine and actor response.

### 2.3 Capacity and allocation

Examples: scarce municipal service slots, repair priority, public venue allocation, limited activity capacity or resource assignment.

This is especially valuable because it creates legible winners/losers and downstream actor choices without requiring a detailed economy.

### 2.4 Service and resource support

Examples: fund/provide/withdraw a bounded service input, repair allocation, public provision or compatible support/subsidy where the material effect is represented.

A hidden budget spreadsheet is not required. Represent costs/resources only where they constrain later visible choices.

### 2.5 Obligation / public commitment

A public promise, municipal duty or explicit institutional obligation can become legitimate information and later evidence of fulfilment/failure. It does not directly set loyalty or resentment.

### 2.6 Institutional sponsorship

Recognise/support an activity, provide a venue/window/resource, call a meeting, launch a bounded public project or endorse/oppose a proposal.

This may alter PA-08 opportunities and information flows while actors retain participation decisions.

### 2.7 Enforcement posture — bounded research family

Warning, temporary closure/removal, referral or enforcement priority may matter where later product scope supports it. PA-12 does **not** create a complete legal/crime simulator.

## 4. Canonical macro → micro → aftermath fixture

Use Bar F01 and the adjacent public-space/activity ecology because B1 already established material/service/activity/player seams there.

### Starting state

- Bar F01 has one normal public operating window and finite compatible social/activity capacity.
- A nearby municipal room/public area has one bounded evening allocation window.
- Antonio and Manolo have different routines/relationships/opportunities.
- Carmen's Bar F01 service role/material state remains owned by PA-07/PA-02.

### Macro decision

The player, through legitimate mayoral authority, grants the municipal room's evening slot to a recurring community card/social activity for a bounded period and provides a small accepted setup/resource allocation.

PA-12 changes only:

```text
public-room access = allowed for the activity in the declared window
activity capacity = available in that window
municipal setup/resource allocation = available
scope + activation + expiry/review condition = explicit
```

### Independent actor responses

- Antonio now has an additional compatible leisure opportunity and may choose it.
- Manolo may refuse because the window conflicts with a real obligation/routine, or may exploit the new opportunity on another day.
- Carmen may gain/lose Bar F01 demand pressure indirectly but remains free to choose within her normal work/service context.
- another eligible actor may dislike the allocation because it displaced an opportunity they used, but that attitude/action must arise from their own knowledge/goals/relationships rather than policy code setting `opposition=true`.

### Third-person aftermath

The player can physically visit the room/bar area and encounter:

- people using or ignoring the new opportunity;
- displaced/changed capacity elsewhere;
- requests/complaints/support from actors who legitimately know/experience the change;
- changed service/activity pressure;
- a PA-10 chain only if real actor decisions/consequences continue it.

### Micro response

The player may help set up, mediate a capacity dispute, personally attend, decline a request, move a legitimate resource, or later propose an amendment/repeal.

Those micro acts enter PA-09/shared owners. There is no hidden `policySuccess += 1` sole authority.

## 5. Same policy, asymmetric citizens

The policy applies one shared rule to all eligible actors. Reactions differ because actor state differs.

Counterfactual pair:

- Antonio knows about the new slot, has free time and values the relevant social opportunity.
- Manolo has a conflicting obligation and only partial information about why the slot changed.

They may therefore choose participate versus decline/seek another time without any `Supporter/Opponent` policy role being assigned by governance.

If two actors have equivalent relevant state, PA-12 does not require arbitrary divergence merely for drama. The claim is that **different owned inputs are allowed to matter**, not that every citizen must react uniquely.

## 6. Macro decision must become third-person play

A policy cannot satisfy PA-12 solely because an office dashboard changes.

For every adopted high-value lever, the product must be capable of exposing at least one normal-town consequence such as:

- changed access/opening;
- people present/absent at a place;
- changed service/activity capacity;
- visible repair/setup/use;
- a legitimate request/complaint/support interaction;
- changed material opportunity;
- a later actor/event consequence.

The office/interventor/document/compact-menu interaction can explain direct intended effects, scope and known constraints. It should **not predict exact downstream NPC choices**.

## 7. Micro behaviour can change implementation

Governance does not end at the decision screen.

Using the canonical fixture:

- helping set up may make an already-authorized activity opportunity actually usable sooner;
- obstructing/removing a required material input may delay it if the action is lawful/possible under PA-09 consequences;
- mediating a conflict may change actor-owned willingness/obligations only through legitimate social owners;
- ignoring repeated complaints may leave a condition unresolved;
- amending the allocation changes future institutional conditions.

These local actions alter real owners. A hidden mayoral “implementation meter” may summarize for UI later, but cannot be the sole causal authority.

## 8. Two divergent trajectories from equivalent start

The amendment requires transformation without ideology flags. Use equivalent starting town state and the **same primitive families**.

### Trajectory A — shared civic provision

Across a bounded horizon the player repeatedly:

- allocates municipal space/time to open community use;
- supports selected service/activity capacity;
- fulfils public commitments;
- resolves some conflicts through restitution/repair rather than exclusion;
- continues local third-person support where useful.

Possible result through existing owners:

- more recurring shared activities/opportunities;
- actors who benefited and legitimately know why may become more willing to cooperate through normal social owners;
- services/routines reorganise around the new opportunities;
- new conflicts can still occur because capacity remains finite.

### Trajectory B — concentrated/selective access

From the same start the player repeatedly:

- allocates the same scarce rooms/resources to a narrow set of uses/actors;
- applies more restrictive access windows;
- withdraws some shared support;
- locally reinforces those choices after encountering opposition.

Possible result:

- different place/activity availability;
- excluded actors substitute, organise, resist or disengage for their own reasons;
- different relationships/obligations/information chains may emerge only where legitimately caused;
- later event/opportunity ecology diverges.

The descriptive labels “communal”, “clientelist”, “highly regulated” or similar may summarize an observed outcome for humans. They are **not simulation authorities**.

## 9. Accidental mistake versus deliberate transformation

PA-12 adopts the cross-cutting boundary:

```text
one low/medium mistaken intervention
 -> bounded consequence
 -> correction / restitution / adaptation path where appropriate

repeated coherent high-impact interventions after legible feedback
 -> stabilizers can be exhausted/overcome
 -> actors/services/institutions reorganise
 -> materially different equilibrium or crisis may persist
```

No `isPlayerTryingToCauseChaos` flag is needed. Persistence/severity of accepted causes is enough.

### Clumsy-player fixture

The player mistakenly allocates the room to an incompatible window once. Before substantial downstream commitments form, the player amends the window. Some inconvenience can remain, but ordinary correction prevents an automatic town-wide collapse.

### Persistent-transformer fixture

The player repeatedly removes/redirects multiple shared access/capacity/resource supports after affected actors have reacted and the game has made consequences legible. Recovery mechanisms may then fail to restore the prior ecology; actors find substitutes, withdraw cooperation, organise or generate new bounded chains. PA-13 may bound propagation, but it may not restore the default town by fiat.

## 10. Bad mayor remains a playable state

Where no specific authored hard fail is logically required, governance failure should alter play rather than invalidate the save merely because the town is worse.

Possible changed conditions include:

- unavailable/degraded services;
- actors refusing cooperation;
- changed access/routines;
- officials/organisations withdrawing support where represented;
- stronger opposition/support networks through legitimate social owners;
- repair/reconciliation opportunities;
- later institutional intervention if future product/legal scope supports it.

This is a product boundary, not a promise that every conceivable destructive action is simulated.

## 11. Repeal is not a time machine

Fixture:

1. the room-allocation policy operates long enough for actors to attend, decline, form obligations, move resources and generate legitimate memories/information;
2. the player repeals/amends the policy;
3. future access/capacity uses the new institutional rule;
4. already-caused attendance, moved resources, fulfilled/broken commitments, relationships, beliefs and memories remain with their normal owners;
5. recovery becomes new gameplay rather than retroactive deletion.

A repeal that resets every affected system to its pre-policy snapshot is a causal failure.

## 12. Governance + PA-10 + PA-11 composition

Governance may create a high-leverage **root condition**, not a prewritten story.

```text
policy changes public-room access/capacity
 -> actors independently encounter it
 -> one legitimate conflict/request/action seeds PA-10 chain
 -> PA-10 bounds/terminates downstream continuation
 -> PA-11 exposes only legitimate traces/testimony/world changes
 -> player returns, learns a partial aftermath and may intervene locally
```

PA-12 does not command the chain. PA-10 does not invent citizens' policy attitudes. PA-11 does not expose the mayor a private truth oracle.

## 13. No dedicated strategy layer by default

The accepted default presentation hypothesis remains:

- conversations with an official/interventor/relevant NPC;
- meetings;
- documents/desk interactions;
- compact conventional/diegetic UI where clarity requires it;
- physical follow-up in the town.

A separate overhead/city-management layer may be justified later only by a concrete usability/scale need. PA-12 does not require it to satisfy systemic governance.

## 14. Negative controls

### N12-1 — Ideology button

FAIL if `CommunistTown`, `AuthoritarianTown`, `CultTown`, `ChaosTown` or equivalent directly drives citizen behaviour in place of concrete rules/opportunities plus actor state.

### N12-2 — Mind-control mayor

FAIL if policy directly sets belief, loyalty, friendship, fear or next actor action.

### N12-3 — Dashboard-only governance

FAIL if only aggregate values change and no service/access/opportunity/material/activity/event/actor-facing consequence exists.

### N12-4 — Script per policy / NPC

FAIL if each policy encodes bespoke responses for named actors rather than exposing shared conditions consumed by normal owners.

### N12-5 — Universal reaction

FAIL if all actors support/oppose merely because the policy carries a global attitude tag.

### N12-6 — Decorative office

FAIL if mayoral choices are dialogue flavour with no shared-state effect.

### N12-7 — One-click apocalypse

FAIL if one ambiguous low/medium action routinely creates irreversible systemic collapse without reinforcing causes.

### N12-8 — Rubber town

FAIL if sustained deliberate pressure is automatically erased so materially different equilibria cannot persist.

### N12-9 — Repeal time machine

FAIL if removing a rule deletes already-caused memories, debts, relationships, transfers or events.

### N12-10 — Mandatory strategy view

FAIL if an overhead mode becomes required despite the accepted governance task being understandable/executable through compact office/in-world interaction and follow-up.

### N12-11 — Opinion-only consequence

FAIL if approval/reputation is the sole result while actual people/services/opportunities remain unchanged.

### N12-12 — Destruction-only freedom

FAIL if the only meaningful town transformation channel is physical destruction/violence.

## 17. Unit conclusion

Governance is valuable precisely because it can alter many people's conditions at once. It remains part of the same Living World only if **citizens still own what they do with those changed conditions**.

Juego2 should let the player build, damage, reform or destabilise a town through sustained concrete causes — not select a labelled town ending from a menu.
