# Topology A — "Dos Orillas" (two-bank river city)

WP: WP-CITY-00
Status: candidate option, constructed before any preference was formed.

## 1. Premise

One substantial river runs NE→SW through the middle of the settlement. The city grew on **both
banks** and has never stopped being two half-towns that need each other.

- **Left bank (SE):** the historic side. Casco viejo on a low rocky shelf, the plaza and town hall
  behind it, the commercial spine running NE along the bank.
- **Right bank (NW):** the working and living side. The larger residential fabric, the workshops,
  the valley road, the bus, and the fluvial landing downstream.

The river is wide enough that crossing is a decision, not a step: ~25–35 m at the old bridge.

## 4. Crossing strategy

| Crossing | Character | Connects | Availability |
|---|---|---|---|
| Puente Viejo | stone, narrow, pedestrian + handcart | casco/plaza ↔ Talleres + right-bank spine | permanent, historic |
| Puente de Abajo | road bridge, vehicles + freight | Entrada/Puerto ↔ left-bank downstream | late production |
| Pasarela de la Vega | wooden footbridge | upstream huertas, both banks | seasonal, flood-closable |

Between the Puente Viejo and the Puente de Abajo there is roughly 450 m of unbridged river. That
gap is the topology's main instrument and its main liability.

## 5. Route structure

Two bank spines connected by two or three crossings: a **ladder with very long rungs**. Loops exist
but each loop is large — reaching a point on the other bank 100 m away can cost 500 m of walking.
Fine-grained route choice lives inside each bank, not between them.

## 6. Axis profile

**Mobility / loops.** Coarse loops, strong commitment. The interesting decision is *which crossing*,
and it is a real decision with real cost. But before the Puente de Abajo exists, the Puente Viejo is
the universal connector for every cross-river trip, which is a funnel in everything but name.

**River / port logic.** The strongest of the three. The river cannot be read as scenery: it is the
reason the city has two halves. The port sits on the road bank, so freight never has to cross water
to be dispatched — the most plausible port logistics of the three options.

**Reactive density.** The weakest. A ~0.40 km² dense fabric split into two banks yields two ~0.20 km²
half-towns. CITY-02 would have to programme systemic anchors twice or accept that one bank is thin.

**Schedules / actor travel.** Highly legible. "He lives across the river" is instantly readable, and
the daily crossing is a strong rhythm. Risk: crossings synchronise everybody's routes, so traffic
concentrates at bridge heads at predictable hours — good for encounters, bad for variety.

**Investigation / following.** Split result. Bridge heads are excellent legitimate observation
points, which serves investigation. But once a follower knows which bridge the target used, the
search space collapses; and the bank spines are long corridors with few lateral escapes.

**Governance.** Excellent. Bridge access, tolls, repair priority and closure are powerful and
immediately felt; quay concessions, per-bank service provision, flood works and market siting all
have obvious macro→micro consequences. Closing a crossing is the single most legible municipal act
available in any of the three options.

**Expansion.** Four credible directions: upstream on either bank, downstream past the port, up-slope
on the right bank. No seam requires moving an existing core street.

**Production cost.** The highest. Two full riverfronts, at least two bridges, a wide permanent water
body, and duplicated service sets. Blockout cost and art-kit cost both roughly double at the edges.

## 8. Expansion seams

1. Upstream right bank → more Ensanche.
2. Upstream left bank → vega and a second huerta quarter.
3. Downstream right bank → port growth, yards, depot.
4. Up-slope right bank → Barrio Alto growth, miradores.
5. (Later) left bank downstream once the Puente de Abajo exists.

## 9. Retained-seed implications

At a corrected seed band of 0.03–0.06 km² the seed cannot hold both banks.

- A **left-bank seed** (plaza + casco + bridge head + bar + river strip) is buildable and beautiful,
  but omits the residential majority, the workshops and the port — i.e. the seed under-represents the
  constitution it is supposed to be a piece of.
- A **straddling seed** (both bridge heads + the bridge itself) is the only representative option and
  is materially more expensive than the other two topologies' seeds.

## 10. Known weaknesses, declared before refutation

- Density is split at exactly the scale band where reactive density is the binding constraint.
- The funnel condition persists until the most expensive piece — the second bridge — is built.
- No representative small seed exists.
- Cross-river micro-movement is impossible, so the city has no cheap, frequent, low-stakes crossing.
