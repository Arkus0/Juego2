# Topology C — "Ribera Larga" (long single-bank riverside city)

WP: WP-CITY-00
Status: candidate option, constructed before any preference was formed.

## 1. Premise

The river runs W→E along the south side of the valley floor. The city occupies the **strip between
the water and the slope**, roughly 950 m long and 250–350 m deep, and has grown lengthwise rather
than outward. Everything is close laterally and far longitudinally.

Reading W (upstream) → E (downstream): casco → plaza → Calle Mayor → Ensanche → talleres → puerto.

Three long parallel routes — **Calle Alta**, **Calle Mayor**, **Paseo Fluvial** — tied together by
frequent short rungs. A ladder, roughly 950 m long.

## 4. Crossing strategy

| Crossing | Character | Connects | Availability |
|---|---|---|---|
| Puente Viejo | stone, historic, narrow | casco ↔ camino sur, ermita, cementerio, valley road | permanent |
| Puente del Muelle | freight road bridge | Puerto ↔ south bank | late production |

Between them lies ~900 m of unbridged river. Ordinary daily life crosses the river **almost never**.

## 5. Route structure

The strongest short-range loop density of the three: three continuous parallel routes with many
rungs means almost every trip has two or three credible variants with genuinely different character —
quiet and high, commercial and middle, waterside and low. The plaza is never mandatory.

But there is one axis. Direction of travel is always obvious, and "the other side of town" always
means "further along".

## 6. Axis profile

**Mobility / loops.** Excellent at short range, monotone at long range. Loops are cheap and frequent;
topological surprise is low.

**River / port logic.** The fault line of this option. The river defines the southern edge, land use,
the port and the whole silhouette — but it is a **boundary the city walks beside**, not one it must
cross. Against the WP's acceptance criterion *"river shapes movement rather than functioning only as
scenery"*, the honest reading is that it shapes **land use** decisively and **movement** only through
its two terminal bridges.

**Reactive density.** The best of the three. The smallest area carries the same content, everything
is within ~300 m laterally, and no service set is duplicated. This is the cheapest city to make feel
genuinely full.

**Schedules / actor travel.** Good. The ladder gives real route-character choices, and the length
gives honest travel time through continuously built fabric rather than through empty filler. "Being
elsewhere in town" works, it just always means "at the other end".

**Investigation / following.** Moderate. Three levels give a target somewhere to go, but a follower
always knows the axis; losing somebody requires a rung, a doorway or a crowd, not a topological
decision. Search space shrinks linearly rather than branching.

**Governance.** Moderate. There is no cheap, frequently used crossing whose closure is a *small*
decision with a quickly walkable consequence — both bridges are terminal, so closing either is
drastic. The available levers (quay concession, market siting, paseo access, slope path maintenance,
opening hours) work, but macro→micro granularity is coarser than in the other options.

**Expansion.** Four directions, but the fourth — south across the river — is qualitatively harder and
effectively converts the city into Topology A.

**Production cost.** The lowest by a clear margin. One riverfront, one bridge initially, linear
phasing that never leaves an orphaned district, and the simplest blockout. Art-kit reuse is highest
because the fabric is continuous.

## 8. Expansion seams

1. **W upstream** → vega, second huerta quarter.
2. **E downstream** → port growth, depot, yards.
3. **N up-slope** → Barrio Alto growth, terraces, miradores.
4. **S across the river** → possible but structurally transformative, not incremental.

## 9. Retained-seed implications

The W end is the natural seed: casco + plaza edge + Puente Viejo + bar + one escalera to the Calle
Alta + a paseo strip. It is representative of the city's *character* and cheap to build, and it
extends purely eastward without touching anything.

This is the most comfortable seed of the three — which is also a warning, because comfort here comes
from the city having only one direction of growth.

## 10. Known weaknesses, declared before refutation

- The river is an edge, not a barrier; ordinary life crosses it almost never.
- One axis: following, searching and "which way did he go" collapse toward binary.
- No cheap, frequently used crossing means governance loses its most granular physical lever.
- Longitudinal monotony makes the "four streets plus scenery" failure mode the nearest one.
