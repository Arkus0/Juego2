# WP-CITY-00 — Refutation log

WP: WP-CITY-00
Purpose: `Docs/workpacks/CITY/WP-CITY-00.md` Work item 4 requires selecting a preferred constitution
**after** the alternatives have been genuinely tested. This log records the attempt to kill all three
options before any preference existed, so that the selection is a survivor rather than a favourite.

Method: each option receives its strongest available charges, then its strongest available defence,
then a verdict naming what it actually loses on. A charge is only counted if it cites a criterion the
workpack, the track README or an inherited direction document actually states.

Order of work: all charges were written before any defence, and all defences before any verdict.

---

## Charges

### Against A — Dos Orillas

**A-1 — Density is split at exactly the wrong scale.**
Track invariant 4 is *"reactive density over acreage: empty expansion is worse than a smaller city
with meaningful locations."* At the corrected dense band (0.30–0.45 km², see `SCALE_ENVELOPE.md`),
A produces two ≈0.15–0.22 km² half-towns. CITY-02 then either programmes systemic anchors twice —
two bars, two shops, two civic touchpoints — or accepts that one bank is visibly thinner than the
other. Both outcomes are worse than one contiguous core of the same total area.

**A-2 — The funnel persists through the whole early production window.**
Track invariant 2 is *"loops over funnels"*, and the WP's negative gate rejects a plan that "puts
every meaningful route through the plaza". A's loops exist only once the **second** crossing exists,
and the second crossing is a road bridge — the single most expensive piece of geometry in the whole
constitution. Until it is built, every cross-river trip uses the Puente Viejo, which sits at the
plaza/casco. The funnel is therefore not a temporary early-seed state; it is the operating condition
for as long as production realistically takes.

**A-3 — No representative small seed exists.**
The WP requires that the *"demo/product seed can occupy a real retained part of the selected
constitution"*. A single-bank seed omits the residential majority, the workshops and the port. A
straddling seed must build two bridge heads and the bridge itself, which is materially more expensive
than either competing option's seed. There is no cheap seed that represents A.

**A-4 — The city has no cheap, frequent crossing.**
Every crossing in A is a major structure. That removes the most granular physical governance lever
available to any river city: a small closure or repair whose consequence is felt within minutes of
walking (`PA-12_AMENDMENT_01` §3.1, §5.3). A's governance is powerful but blunt.

### Against B — Cuña de Confluencia

**B-1 — The port risks becoming a mission appendix.**
`WP-CITY-01` acceptance requires that *"port and rural edge participate in ordinary movement, not
only story missions"*, and the WP's negative gate rejects using the port as a decorative label. In B
the port sits downstream, outside the wedge. If the only reasons to go are freight and story, no
ordinary actor goes, the ~550 m walk is paid only by the player on errands, and the district is
decoration with a job title. **This is the strongest charge against B and it is not answerable by
intent.**

**B-2 — The terrace can silently become the only through route.**
B claims three continuous longitudinal routes. In practice the Calle Mayor terrace is the flattest,
widest and cheapest to build; the paseo is at the bottom of a bank and the callejas altas are stairs.
Under production pressure the two hard ones get built in fragments, and B collapses into a
single-spine town with a scenic riverside — which is charge A-2 wearing a different hat.

**B-3 — Three levels and two watercourses is the hardest thing to read and to build.**
Verticality multiplies stairs, retaining walls, ramps and readability problems, and the wedge asks
the player to hold a three-level mental model. `WP-CITY-04` must validate verticality, sightlines and
followability; B is the option most likely to fail that validation, and a failure there is expensive
because it invalidates level placement rather than dressing.

**B-4 — The navigability premise is invented.**
A Liébana mountain river does not carry loaded working craft. As first drafted, B's port depended on a
fictional claim that below the confluence the joined river is floatable for shallow craft toward the
coast. `WP-ART-00` owns setting direction and `Docs/workpacks/CITY/README.md` forbids CITY from
silently restyling the game. B may not assume this premise into existence.

*This charge was correct and was underestimated. See the repair-cycle-1 note under its verdict.*

**B-5 — The wedge tip concentrates identity in the most expensive fabric.**
Casco, plaza, old bridge, mirador and the port approach all meet at the tip. That is where the
irregular lanes, the level changes and the overhanging houses are — the costliest kit in the project
and the hardest to author. B front-loads its most expensive geometry.

### Against C — Ribera Larga

**C-1 — The river is scenery by the WP's own test.**
WP acceptance states plainly: *"River shapes movement rather than functioning only as scenery."* In C
the river is crossed at the two ends and nowhere else; ordinary daily life crosses it essentially
never. C shapes **land use** with the river, which is real, but the criterion is about movement. On a
literal reading, C fails an acceptance criterion rather than scoring badly on an axis.

**C-2 — One axis collapses follow/search gameplay.**
`WP-CITY-01` acceptance requires *"follow/search gameplay has enough branching to be gameplay, not a
corridor"*, and `PA-11` legibility needs the player to be able to lose and re-find people through
world structure. In C, direction is always known and the search space shrinks linearly. Level changes
give variety of *character*, not of *destination*.

**C-3 — Governance loses its small lever.**
Both of C's crossings are terminal, so every crossing decision is drastic. PA-12's most valuable
macro↔micro loop — a small rule change whose effect the player walks into minutes later — has no
cheap physical carrier.

**C-4 — The nearest failure mode is the workpack's own first negative gate.**
The WP fails a plan that *"is effectively four streets plus scenery"*. C's honest description is
three long parallel streets plus a riverside and a slope. It is not that C *is* that failure, but
that C is the option for which the failure requires the least drift.

## Defences

### For A

The two-bank city is the most literal and most honest reading of the track's own permanent
ingredient: *"river as a genuine movement/land-use boundary"*. A river you must decide how to cross
is structurally stronger than one you walk beside. A-2 is answerable by putting the **pasarela**
(cheap footbridge) rather than the road bridge into early production, which restores a second
crossing at low cost. A-1 is answerable by declaring the right bank deliberately lower-intensity, in
which case the split is a designed density gradient rather than a dilution. A-4 is then answered too,
because the pasarela is exactly the cheap, closable, flood-prone crossing that governance wants.

### For B

B-1 is answerable **structurally**, not by promise, and the answer is the only one that counts: keep
the road junction and the bus stop adjacent to the port so every arrival and departure in the city
passes it; site at least two everyday non-port services there; and make the towpath the pleasant
walking route rather than only the working one. If those hold, the port is on ordinary routes by
construction.

B-2 is answerable by making route continuity a **permanent invariant** rather than an aspiration, so
a production phase that leaves only the terrace is a contract breach and not a schedule decision.

B-3 is partly a false charge: verticality is already mandated by the setting (`SETTING.md`,
`VISUAL_BIBLE.md`) and by `PRODUCTION_BLUEPRINT.md` §1.2's Barrio Alto and stepped lanes. It is a
cost every option carries. What is attributable to B is the *amount* of level change, which can be
capped and named as a CITY-04 measurement risk.

B-4 **is** answerable here, and the first draft of this log was wrong to say otherwise. The answer is
not to argue the premise or to defer it, but to remove the need for it: a landing justified by timber
rafted at high water, áridos worked off the bars, a roped ferry and road break-bulk requires only that
the water below a confluence be wide and slow, which is hydrology rather than setting. Everything
systemically valuable about the district survives that swap, and the city stops being a node on a
long-distance trade route — a real reduction, stated rather than hidden.

B-5 is real, but it is also where the project already intended to start (`PRODUCTION_BLUEPRINT.md`
§1.4 seed = plaza side + old bridge + bar + old-quarter connection + river edge). B does not add that
cost; it inherits it.

### For C

C is by a clear margin the cheapest city to finish, the densest per square metre, and the easiest to
phase without orphaning a district. Track invariant 4 says a smaller city with meaningful locations
beats empty expansion — read strictly, that argues *for* C. Its ladder genuinely delivers loops, and
its seed is the most comfortable of the three. If the project's real risk is never finishing, C is
the rational answer.

C-1 is partly answerable: a river that determines where every street can go, where work happens, how
the city is entered and what the silhouette is, is not scenery in any ordinary sense. The criterion
can be read as "the river must be structural", and under that reading C passes.

## Verdicts

### A — Dos Orillas: rejected, but conditionally

A survives as a coherent city. It loses on **A-1 and A-3 together**: at the corrected scale band it
cannot be both dense and representable by a small seed. The pasarela defence genuinely answers A-2
and A-4, but it cannot answer A-3, because no cheap seed shows a two-bank city.

Recorded explicitly, because it matters: **A's rejection is coupled to the scale decision.** If the
dense band were later raised above roughly 0.7 km², A-1 dissolves (each bank reaches critical mass on
its own) and A becomes the strongest option. A is therefore not rejected on quality; it is rejected as
the wrong shape for the size this city is going to be. If the size changes, the constitution should be
reopened rather than stretched.

### C — Ribera Larga: rejected

C's defence of C-1 is the best available and it is still not enough. The workpack does not say "the
river must be structural"; it says the river must shape **movement** rather than function only as
scenery, and it separately requires a crossing strategy. A city whose inhabitants cross the river
twice a year satisfies the land-use half and not the movement half. Adding a mid-town crossing to fix
this gives the far bank something to be — and that is Topology A.

C-2 and C-3 compound it: the option that is cheapest to build is also the one that gives the least
back to the two systems this project is actually betting on, investigation and governance. The cost
argument is real and is preserved as a standing constraint on the selected option, not as a reason to
select C.

### B — Cuña de Confluencia: survives, with three charges converted into obligations

B is not selected because it scored best; it is selected because it is the only option whose charges
are answerable **by structure** rather than by hoping. Two of its answers must be permanent:

1. **B-1 → invariant.** The arrival/road/bus junction stays adjacent to the port, and at least two
   everyday non-port services live there. Without this, B's port is decoration and B should lose.
2. **B-2 → invariant.** All three longitudinal routes stay continuous end to end. A phase that leaves
   only the terrace breaks the constitution.
3. **B-4 → invariant (repaired in cycle 1).** Originally this was recorded as an open question
   referred to `WP-ART-00`, with the admission that a refusal would reopen the topology. Independent
   review [#5261386416](https://github.com/Arkus0/Juego2/pull/65#pullrequestreview-5261386416) FAILed
   the candidate for exactly that: a constitution whose Definition of Done is stability for CITY-01
   cannot close over a condition capable of invalidating it, and the deferral had also inverted the
   CITY→ART authority direction.

   The error was treating the charge as a binary — assume the premise or defer it — when a third
   option existed. CSI-05 now states that the landing depends on no premise beyond the inherited
   setting, and §2.5 of the constitution gives four reasons for the landing that need none.

B-3 and B-5 are accepted as declared risks: verticality is capped and named for CITY-04 measurement,
and the expensive tip is where production was already going to start.

### What the survivor does not get for free

B beats A and C on mobility, investigation, governance granularity and port plausibility. It does not
beat C on cost or on density-per-square-metre, and it does not beat A on the raw force of the river
as a barrier. Those two losses stay visible in the selected constitution as the constraints they are:
the cost discipline C argued for is carried into the scale envelope, and A's barrier logic is carried
into the invariant that the main river has exactly one historic crossing in the core.
