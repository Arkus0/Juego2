# WP-CITY-00 — Alternative-topology comparison matrix

WP: WP-CITY-00
Covers the union of the eight axes required for this comparison and the ten evaluation criteria in
`Docs/workpacks/CITY/WP-CITY-00.md` Work item 3.

Ratings are **Strong / Adequate / Weak**, each with the reason that produced it. A rating is a
comparison between these three options at the selected scale band, not an absolute score.

## 1. Evaluation matrix

| Criterion | A — Dos Orillas | B — Cuña de Confluencia | C — Ribera Larga |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Route loops vs bottlenecks** | **Weak→Adequate.** Loops exist only once the second crossing is built; until then the Puente Viejo is the universal connector. Loop grain is coarse. | **Strong.** Three designed longitudinal route families plus four lateral Arroyo crossings, two Río crossings per state and the casco lane web; fine-grained alternatives locally and a territorial loop across the main river. | **Strong at short range, Weak at long range.** Ladder loops are cheap and frequent, but there is one axis and direction is always obvious. |
| **Mobility / route choice quality** | **Adequate.** One high-stakes decision (which bridge), few low-stakes ones. | **Strong.** Two crossing economies — Arroyo crossed casually/often, Río crossed rarely/deliberately — plus state-dependent landing access. | **Adequate.** Choices are about *character* (high/commercial/waterside), not about *destination*. |
| **District identity** | **Strong.** Bank membership is the identity; "the other side" is unambiguous. | **Strong.** Tip/terrace/slope/across-Arroyo/port-bank/downstream give legible identities without labels. | **Adequate.** Identity is positional (west end / middle / east end) and shades into neighbours. |
| **River + crossing logic** | **Strong.** The river is why the city has two halves; it cannot be read as scenery. | **Strong.** Two watercourses have deliberately different roles, and the corrected port bank makes the Río a genuine territorial boundary rather than a decorative edge. | **Weak.** The river governs land use decisively but is crossed at the two ends only; ordinary life almost never crosses it. |
| **Port plausibility and usefulness** | **Strong plausibility, Adequate usefulness.** Port is on the road bank so freight never crosses water; but it sits at one end of a bank spine. | **Strong.** Below-confluence hydrology supports timber/áridos/ferry work without a long-distance navigability premise. Puerto + Entrada share the Orilla-sur bank, giving two dry approaches (valley road and camino sur) plus the direct core crossing X6/X7. It is a route node rather than an impossible dry continuation of the Wedge. | **Strong plausibility, Adequate usefulness.** Port at the road junction is convincing, but it is a terminus, not a hub. |
| **Likely reactive-location density** | **Weak.** 0.30–0.45 km² split into two half-towns; systemic anchors must be programmed twice or one bank stays thin. | **Adequate→Strong.** High-intensity core remains contiguous on the Wedge; the Ensanche and compact Puerto/Entrada edge sit across separate water boundaries but are deliberately lower-intensity rather than second civic centres. | **Strong.** Smallest area for the same content, no duplicated service sets, everything within ~300 m laterally. |
| **Actor schedule / travel plausibility** | **Strong but synchronised.** "Lives across the river" is instantly readable; risk is that crossings synchronise routes. | **Strong and varied.** Short Arroyo crossings give local routine, port shifts create a real cross-Río commute, Puente Viejo gives the rural trip, stairs give vertical routine. | **Adequate.** Real route-character variety and honest travel time, but "elsewhere" always means "further along". |
| **Follow / search / investigation value** | **Adequate.** Bridge heads are excellent vantage, but once the crossing is known the search space collapses. | **Strong.** A target can change level, Arroyo crossing or Río crossing; a port-bound target can take X1 + camino sur or the landing-side X6/X7 when available. | **Adequate→Weak.** Search space shrinks linearly; losing somebody needs a doorway or crowd, not a topological decision. |
| **Mayoral access / routing consequences** | **Strong but blunt.** Bridge access, tolls, repair priority and closure are powerful — and every one is drastic. | **Strong and granular.** Arroyo crossings are cheap/frequent/closable; ferry hours/fare, X7 repair, quay concessions and market siting add larger levers. | **Adequate.** Both crossings are terminal, so crossing decisions are all-or-nothing; remaining levers are real but coarse. |
| **Ability to preserve a retained product seed** | **Weak.** No cheap seed represents a two-bank city: one bank omits too much; a straddling seed must build bridgeheads and bridge. | **Strong.** Wedge-tip seed carries four district families and the landing-side crossing head as the visible seam toward Puerto across the joined river. | **Strong.** The west end is the most comfortable seed of the three, extending purely eastward. |
| **Expansion cost** | **Adequate.** Four directions, but one whole bank waits on the road bridge. | **Strong.** Six directions remain; the cheapest large residential expansion is Ensanche, while port-bank growth and X6→X7 are phaseable independently. | **Adequate.** Three cheap directions plus one south-river transformation. |
| **Production cost** | **Weak.** Two full riverfronts, ≥2 bridges, wide permanent water, duplicated service sets. | **Adequate.** Cheap Arroyo geometry with high value-per-metre, one later expensive Río bridge, small phaseable port, level-change kit cost. | **Strong.** One riverfront, one bridge initially, linear phasing, highest kit reuse, simplest blockout. |

## 3. What the comparison actually decided

No option wins every row, and selection did not go to the highest row count.

- **C wins cost and density outright** and still loses, because it is at risk on a stated river/movement
  criterion and gives the least back to investigation and governance.
- **A wins raw river force and is strong on governance** and still loses, because at this scale band it
  splits density and has no representative small seed.
- **B wins mobility, investigation, governance granularity and seed preservation**, is second on cost
  and density, and remains the only option whose charges are answerable by explicit structure.

Cycle 4 changes B's bank embedding, not the selection rationale: the port is now physically on
Orilla sur and the Río crossing is explicit. C's cost discipline remains in the scale envelope; A's
barrier logic remains in CSI-06.
