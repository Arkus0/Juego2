# WP-CITY-00 — Alternative-topology comparison matrix

WP: WP-CITY-00
Covers the union of the eight axes required for this comparison and the ten evaluation criteria in
`Docs/workpacks/CITY/WP-CITY-00.md` Work item 3.

Ratings are **Strong / Adequate / Weak**, each with the reason that produced it. A rating is a
comparison between these three options at the selected scale band, not an absolute score.

## 1. Evaluation matrix

| Criterion | A — Dos Orillas | B — Cuña de Confluencia | C — Ribera Larga |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Route loops vs bottlenecks** | **Weak→Adequate.** Loops exist only once the second crossing is built; until then the Puente Viejo is the universal connector. Loop grain is coarse (500 m to reach a point 100 m away). | **Strong.** Three continuous longitudinal routes plus four lateral arroyo crossings plus the casco lane web; fine-grained alternatives everywhere. | **Strong at short range, Weak at long range.** Ladder loops are cheap and frequent, but there is one axis and direction is always obvious. |
| **Mobility / route choice quality** | **Adequate.** One high-stakes decision (which bridge), few low-stakes ones. | **Strong.** Two crossing economies — the arroyo crossed casually and often, the río crossed rarely and deliberately. | **Adequate.** Choices are about *character* (high/commercial/waterside), not about *destination*. |
| **District identity** | **Strong.** Bank membership is the identity; "the other side" is unambiguous. | **Strong.** Tip/terrace/slope/across-the-arroyo/downstream give five legible identities without labels. | **Adequate.** Identity is positional (west end / middle / east end) and shades into its neighbours. |
| **River + crossing logic** | **Strong.** The river is why the city has two halves; it cannot be read as scenery. | **Strong.** Two watercourses with deliberately different roles: daily movement vs territorial movement. | **Weak.** The river governs land use decisively but is crossed at the two ends only; ordinary life almost never crosses it. |
| **Port plausibility and usefulness** | **Strong plausibility, Adequate usefulness.** Port is on the road bank so freight never crosses water; but it sits at one end of a bank spine. | **Strong.** Below a confluence is where a shallow valley river first carries loaded craft; three approaches (towpath, quay road, Cuesta) keep it off a cul-de-sac. **Conditional on the ART navigability question.** | **Strong plausibility, Adequate usefulness.** Port at the road junction is convincing, but it is a terminus, not a hub. |
| **Likely reactive-location density** | **Weak.** 0.30–0.45 km² split into two half-towns; systemic anchors must be programmed twice or one bank stays thin. | **Adequate→Strong.** The dense core stays contiguous; only the deliberately lower-intensity Ensanche is across water. | **Strong.** Smallest area for the same content, no duplicated service sets, everything within ~300 m laterally. |
| **Actor schedule / travel plausibility** | **Strong but synchronised.** "Lives across the river" is instantly readable; risk is that crossings synchronise everybody's routes at predictable hours. | **Strong and varied.** Short arroyo crossings give constant local traffic, the port shift gives one long commute, the Puente Viejo gives the rural trip, the stairs give vertical routine. | **Adequate.** Real route-character variety and honest travel time, but "elsewhere" always means "further along". |
| **Follow / search / investigation value** | **Adequate.** Bridge heads are excellent vantage, but once the crossing is known the search space collapses, and the bank spines are corridors. | **Strong.** A follower must commit to a level and the target can change level; the mirador and terrace edge give legitimate vantage without an omniscient overlay. | **Adequate→Weak.** Search space shrinks linearly; losing somebody needs a doorway or a crowd, not a topological decision. |
| **Mayoral access / routing consequences** | **Strong but blunt.** Bridge access, tolls, repair priority and closure are powerful — and every one of them is drastic. | **Strong and granular.** Arroyo crossings are cheap, frequent and closable, so a *small* municipal decision has a consequence walkable in minutes; quay concessions, market siting and the Cuesta sit above that. | **Adequate.** Both crossings are terminal, so crossing decisions are all-or-nothing; remaining levers (quay, market, paseo access, hours) are real but coarse. |
| **Ability to preserve a retained product seed** | **Weak.** No cheap seed represents a two-bank city: a one-bank seed omits residential majority, workshops and port; a straddling seed must build two bridgeheads and the bridge. | **Strong.** The wedge tip seed carries four district families and a visible port seam — and is the blueprint's existing seed plus an arroyo crossing. | **Strong.** The west end is the most comfortable seed of the three, extending purely eastward. (Comfort here comes from having only one growth direction.) |
| **Expansion cost** | **Adequate.** Four directions, but one whole bank waits on the road bridge. | **Strong.** Five directions; the cheapest large residential expansion (Ensanche, across a short span) is also the easiest. | **Adequate.** Three cheap directions plus one (south across the river) that is transformative rather than incremental. |
| **Production cost** | **Weak.** Two full riverfronts, ≥2 bridges, wide permanent water, duplicated service sets. | **Adequate.** Cheap arroyo geometry with high value-per-metre, one expensive bridge, a small phaseable port; level change adds stair/retaining kit. | **Strong.** One riverfront, one bridge initially, linear phasing, highest kit reuse, simplest blockout. |

## 3. What the comparison actually decided

No option wins every row, and the selection did not go to the highest row count.

- **C wins cost and density outright** and still loses, because it is at risk on a stated acceptance
  criterion (river shapes movement) and gives the least back to investigation and governance.
- **A wins river force and is strong on governance** and still loses, because at this scale band it
  splits density and has no representative small seed.
- **B wins mobility, investigation, governance granularity and seed preservation**, is second on cost
  and density, and is the only option whose charges are answerable by structure rather than by intent.

The two losses B carries are preserved as constraints rather than forgotten: C's cost discipline is
carried into the scale envelope, and A's barrier logic is carried into the invariant that the main
river keeps exactly one historic crossing in the core.
